# Introduction

Players rarely remember mechanics.

They remember stories.

Nobody remembers:

"The AI used State Machine #7."

They remember:

"We almost didn't make it home."

Every system in this project exists to create moments worth telling.

---

# Experience #1
## The Briefing

The squad joins the server.

No one knows what tonight's mission will be.

They gather at the FOB.

Load equipment.

Check weather.

Study maps.

Review intelligence.

Decide how to approach the operation.

The mission begins before the helicopter lifts off.

**Goal**

Create anticipation.

---

# Experience #2
## The Quiet Ride

The helicopter lifts off.

Nobody is talking much.

Everyone is checking gear.

The pilot calls out landmarks.

The LZ gets closer.

Someone whispers,

> "This feels like a bad idea."

Everyone laughs.

Then nobody laughs anymore.

---

# Experience #3
## The Infiltration

The objective is visible.

The enemy is unaware.

Players begin communicating quietly.

Nobody wants to fire first.

Suppressors matter.

Night vision matters.

Recon matters.

Patience matters.

The tension should be greater than the firefight.

---

# Experience #4
## Everything Goes Wrong

Someone gets spotted.

Radio chatter begins.

Vehicles start moving.

Searchlights appear.

Helicopters launch.

The squad immediately abandons the original plan.

Nobody complains.

This is the fun part.

---

# Experience #5
## Improvisation

Plans fail.

Players adapt.

The best stories happen here.

Examples:

A bridge becomes the extraction point.

The sniper becomes the medic.

A supply truck becomes transportation.

An abandoned APC becomes cover.

Nothing is scripted.

Everything feels earned.

---

# Experience #6
## The Escape

Mission complete.

Nobody wants another firefight.

Everyone wants to leave alive.

The helicopter arriving should feel like rescue.

Not transportation.

Extraction is a reward.

---

# Experience #7
## The Debrief

Back at base.

Players remove gear.

Discuss mistakes.

Celebrate lucky moments.

Laugh.

Replay events.

This conversation is part of the game.

The mission is not over until the stories begin.

---

# Experience #8
## The Next Day

Players log back in.

Something is different.

The bridge is still destroyed.

Friendly forces advanced.

Enemy patrols changed.

The radar is still offline.

The world remembers.

Players realize...

Their actions mattered.

---

# Experience #9
## The Discovery

Players stumble onto something unexpected.

An abandoned camp.

Enemy documents.

A hidden tunnel.

A convoy not mentioned in the briefing.

A radio transmission.

The best discoveries are optional.

Not mandatory.

---

# Experience #10
## The Impossible Choice

The squad cannot accomplish everything.

Examples:

Rescue civilians.

OR

Destroy the radar.

Capture the officer.

OR

Protect the convoy.

Every decision has consequences.

Players should debate.

There should not always be a correct answer.

---

# Experience #11
## The "Remember When..."

The project succeeds when conversations begin with:

"Remember when..."

Examples:

Remember when we accidentally shot the fuel truck?

Remember when we stole the enemy helicopter?

Remember when the QRF arrived thirty seconds before extraction?

Remember when we thought we'd lost Jake?

Remember when we found the underground bunker?

These stories become the real progression system.

---

# Experience #12
## The Living War

Players should occasionally stop...

...and simply watch.

Friendly helicopters crossing the valley.

Artillery in the distance.

A convoy moving across the map.

Smoke rising from a town.

Two AI patrols fighting without player involvement.

The world should never feel like it only exists for the players.

---

# Experience #13
## Competence

The game should reward preparation.

Recon.

Planning.

Equipment.

Communication.

Discipline.

The squad should slowly become professionals.

Not because their character leveled up...

Because the players did.

---

# Experience #14
## Hope

Even after disaster...

There should always be another plan.

Another route.

Another extraction.

Another objective.

Players should rarely think:

"The mission is over."

Instead:

"We can still pull this off."

---

# Experience #15
## The Friday Night Test

The ultimate measure of success.

Three friends finish playing.

Instead of logging off...

Someone says:

> "One more mission?"

If that happens...

The project succeeded.

---

# The Golden Rule

Players do not remember systems.

Players remember moments.

Every feature in this repository exists to create moments worth remembering.

If we build memorable experiences...

The systems underneath will have done their job.
